# Recorded audio foundation

The former PCM synthesizer is replaced by 36 locally bundled CC0 recordings and a sample-based mixer. The pre-change findings are in [the audio audit](audio-audit.md). The [complete sound design guide](sound-design.md) describes integration and the remaining recording brief.

`src/game/audio/assets.ts` defines supplied files and explicitly requested recordings. `public/audio/credits.json` is the distributed per-file provenance and editing register; `public/audio/LICENSE.txt` provides a human-readable notice. Supplied clips are derived from recordings by Vehicle, Ben Jaszczak and Brian Nelson / Still North Media, Thimras, Jaturo, Max_Headroom and SKrafft. Their primary source and licence links, original filenames, hashes, trims and measurements are retained in the register. The whole encoded palette is approximately 3.6 MB. Freesound preview sources are identified as previews, and the Australian park ambience is identified as an environmental proxy.

`SampleBank.prepare()` fetches local recordings without opening an audio device. `GameAudio.unlock()` resumes the existing gesture-unlocked Web Audio context and decodes the recordings once. A failed primary recording stays silent; secondary layers can be omitted. Failure diagnostics are available through `GameAudio.diagnostics`, alongside a list of requested but unfilled recordings. There is no procedural-tone fallback.

`SoundCue` retains stable event IDs and adds a channel, priority, overlap allowance and authored sample layers. Each layer specifies sample choices, gain and optional delay, timing variation, bounded playback-rate variation, filtering and tail duration. Instruments retain their recorded pitch. Shuffled bags avoid consecutive repeated takes, including at bag boundaries. Sample and event gain budgets leave headroom before the shared output compressor.

The mixer limits event groups, combat groups, sample sources and environmental crossfades separately. Source limits count every layer and outgoing fade. Per-cue cooldowns and a low-priority rate budget prevent dense combat from sounding every collision. High-priority feedback may replace a quieter low-priority event. `reset()` disconnects sources immediately while retaining decoded recordings and the context; `destroy()` also releases caches and closes the device.

Four independently adjustable buses cover music, ambience, combat and interface. Long wind, water and fire recordings use shuffled selection, random entry offsets and overlapping fades. Their colour and level follow the current biome and combat activity; fire follows proximity to camp. Movement and cart presentation clocks advance only from scene-owned active seconds. Planning can retain a quiet camp bed while world movement sounds remain gated.

The five reference events are bow release, enemy impact, spiritual force, parchment interaction and steppe wind. Other events provisionally arrange those same recorded materials. The manifest reserves horse breath, vocalisation and soft-surface contacts, fabric, grass, local wildlife, dedicated stone/rope/thunder/breath recordings, and three recorded music stems. These requests are intentionally silent until supplied, with no simulated morin khuur or synthetic vocal substitute.

Run `python3 scripts/prepare-audio.py /path/to/raw-sources` to edit the original recordings with FFmpeg. This tool never downloads anything or generates a waveform. Unit tests verify the recorded file register, headroom, sample selection, missing files, mixing, overload, phase gating and cleanup. Browser/native checks verify the actual decoder and scene integration.
